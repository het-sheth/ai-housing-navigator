# Feasibility pillars and geographic scope

Research checked September 26, 2026. Requested bounded Sol research and Luna source audit. Findings inform product design; no new rule coverage is implemented.

## Geography audit

The source inventory consists of eight original files: the participant packet, three track briefs, the systems research PDF, a separate original research Markdown file, and two byte-identical catalog CSVs. The audit used the complete page-marked text for the PDFs, the separate research text and one copy of the duplicated catalog. No unreadable sources were reported. It audited geography and coverage statements; it did not validate every linked dataset's actual records.

All three track briefs require at least one Pittsburgh or Allegheny County use case. Track 1: ../ai-housing-hackathon-wiki/raw/markdown/track-1-feasibility.md, page 2, line 42 (path relative to app repository root). The participant packet's page 2 describes examples from Pittsburgh and Allegheny County. Neither establishes a requirement for every municipality or multiple counties.

The catalog combines City, county, state, national and global sources. These data footprints are not product coverage requirements. Its municipal-code entry discusses Allegheny County jurisdictions without naming an additional required county. Track 2 and Track 3 also do not establish a named multi-county scope for Track 1.

The systems research uses US and Pennsylvania context and Pittsburgh as a laboratory. Its recommended demo emphasizes three project archetypes rather than citywide coverage (raw/markdown/housing-systems-report.md, lines 1507-1526). The separate original research text gives the same recommendation around line 1064. These are research recommendations, not organizer requirements.

Conclusion: countywide support can be a product decision, but the event does not require it. The user has requested broad geographic support; distinguish that preference from the audited official requirement. Do not silently reduce the requested scope based on this audit.

## Proposed product pillars

The official track highlights zoning, environmental, infrastructure and policy barriers. No exhaustive seven- or eight-pillar standard was identified. The following is a product taxonomy informed by external practice:

| Pillar | Included topics |
| --- | --- |
| Control and property rights | Site control, acquisition, easements, parcel assembly |
| Land use and design permission | Use, units, dimensions, overlays and applicable design requirements |
| Site and building condition | Survey, topography, soils, structure, existing building suitability |
| Environmental, health and climate hazards | Contamination, flooding, mining and applicable health/resilience issues |
| Utilities and access | Water, sewer, drainage, power, transport and site access |
| Market demand and affordability goals | Intended users, demand, rents/sales evidence, affordability and program fit |
| Capital and operating viability | Development costs, sources of funds, financing, operating expenses and reserves |
| Approvals and delivery readiness | Review sequence, responsible parties, sponsor capacity, contractors and schedule |

The change from the earlier seven explicitly separates demand/affordability from funding and makes continuing operations visible. These topics do not imply a financial engine is implemented. Apply checks according to project scope, jurisdiction and funding; do not require a multifamily program checklist for every home repair.

Keep three independent dimensions: pillar (topic), project stage (when), and check status/evidence provenance (what is known). Permission is a substantive requirement; approvals/delivery describes the route to resolving it. Avoid counting one issue twice in a score. Task completion and favorable evidence are different states.

Weather belongs to long-term hazard exposure and, where relevant, short-term construction scheduling. Politics should be represented through documented policy changes, hearings, stakeholder commitments and funding processes. Do not invent approval odds or infer political preferences of individuals.

## Primary-source basis and limits

- PHFA multifamily application package lists site, market/needs, development scope, environmental, utility, funding, developer experience and operating-budget materials: https://iris.phfa.org/mhp/developers/housingapplication.aspx . Its requirements apply to the relevant programs, not all housing projects.
- HUD HOME FAQs discuss market assessment, underwriting, developer capacity and funding commitments, with exceptions for some activities: https://www.hud.gov/hud-partners/community-affordable-housing-programs/home-faqs . HOME requirements are conditional on program applicability.
- Pittsburgh planning processes distinguish location and proposal-dependent review: https://www.pittsburghpa.gov/Business-Development/City-Planning/Zoning/Planning-Applications-and-Processes . These are City processes, not all county municipalities' procedures.
- Pittsburgh Building Development Application covers several work types: https://www.pittsburghpa.gov/Business-Development/Permits-Licenses-and-Inspections/Permitting/Building-Development-Application . This does not prove automatic evaluation of each type.
- Pittsburgh Water permit guidance: https://www.pgh2o.com/developers-contractors-vendors/permits . Utility service and capacity require the relevant provider's review.
- EPA All Appropriate Inquiries: https://www.epa.gov/brownfields/brownfields-all-appropriate-inquiries . Environmental inquiry is distinct from assuming public-map silence means a clean site.
